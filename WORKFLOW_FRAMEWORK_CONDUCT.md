# Workflow, Framework & Conduct

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the working framework for Team Alpha throughout the FlavorCraft Labs Agency 2 project.
The framework is based on:

- Agency 2 Course Assignment
- Agency 2 Assessment and Grading Criteria
- Module 1 – Onboarding onto an Existing Codebase
- Module 2 – Git Workflow & Advanced GitHub Functions
- Module 3 – GitFlow, Branching, Staging & Deployment
- FlavorCraft Labs Client Brief
- Product Owner Facilitation & Governance Guide

The objective is to support teamwork, communication, adaptability, problem-solving, individual participation and reflective practice throughout the project.

## 2. Assessment Focus

The Agency 2 assessment focuses on how each team member contributes within their assigned role rather than technical perfection alone.
Team members are expected to demonstrate:

- Adherence to agile practices.
- Effective collaboration and communication.
- Problem-solving and conflict resolution.
- Individual participation and accountability.
- Documentation and reporting.
- Adaptability when encountering unfamiliar technologies.
- Risk identification and management.
- Contribution to project delivery.
- Continuous improvement and reflective practice.

Challenges and technical difficulties should be communicated openly and handled collaboratively.

## 3. Team Roles & Responsibilities

### Product Owner

- Defines the product vision and priorities.
- Clarifies business requirements.
- Reviews user stories and acceptance criteria.
- Approves the active sprint backlog.
- Provides feedback and signs off on sprint deliverables.

### Scrum Master

- Facilitates agile meetings and ceremonies.
- Manages and coordinates the project board.
- Supports effective team collaboration.
- Monitors progress and team well-being.
- Identifies and facilitates the removal of blockers.
- Communicates progress, risks and issues to the Product Owner.
- Facilitates continuous improvement.

### Developers

- Establish and maintain the technical implementation.
- Develop assigned features and functionality.
- Integrate the Noroff Recipe Book API.
- Implement routing and application state.
- Write maintainable and modular code.
- Collaborate with design, QA and SEO.
- Communicate technical progress and blockers.
- Document relevant technical decisions.

### Lead Designer & Researcher

- Conduct customer interviews.
- Develop user personas and research findings.
- Establish the visual identity and layout system.
- Create and maintain Figma designs.
- Collaborate with developers during implementation.
- Review visual fidelity and usability.
- Support data-driven design refinements.

### Quality Assurance

- Establish and maintain the testing strategy.
- Conduct manual or automated testing.
- Verify functionality and acceptance criteria.
- Test responsive layouts and edge cases.
- Identify and document defects.
- Review pull requests.
- Provide QA sign-off before merging into main.

### SEO & Analytics Lead

- Establish and implement SEO requirements.
- Integrate analytics and heatmapping tools.
- Manage dynamic metadata and Open Graph.
- Implement Recipe JSON-LD structured data.
- Conduct Lighthouse audits.
- Analyse user behaviour and communicate findings.

### Communication Expectations

All team members are expected to:

- Communicate progress, blockers and completed work.
- Participate in agreed sprint activities.
- Keep relevant team members informed.
- Communicate delays and changes in availability.

- Document significant decisions.

## 5. Sprint Framework

The project consists of three active one-week development sprints.

| Sprint | Objective |
| --- | --- |
| Sprint 1 | Discovery, Research & Core Architecture |
| Sprint 2 | Advanced Features, SEO & Analytics |
| Sprint 3 | Data-Driven Refinement, Optimisation & Final Launch |

### Sprint Ceremonies

| Ceremony | Schedule | Duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sprint Planning | Tuesday AM | 25–30 minutes |
| Stand-ups | Agreed team schedule | Brief updates |
| Mid-Sprint Check-in | Wednesday / Thursday | Asynchronous |
| Sprint Review & Demo | Friday PM | 15–20 minutes |
| Sprint Retrospective | Friday PM | 25 minutes |

## 6. Sprint Planning

The Scrum Master and team prepare the proposed sprint backlog for Product Owner review.
Sprint planning should include:

- Sprint objectives.
- User stories.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Task estimations.
- Task allocation.
- Dependencies.
- Available team capacity.
- Potential risks and blockers.

The Product Owner reviews the proposed backlog, verifies that estimations and acceptance criteria are realistic, and formally approves the active sprint backlog.

## 7. Stand-ups

Stand-ups provide visibility into individual progress and potential blockers.

Each team member should answer:

1. What have I completed since the previous stand-up?
2. What will I work on next?
3. Do I have any blockers or require assistance?

Updates should be concise, relevant and accurate.

The Scrum Master uses these updates to identify dependencies, risks and opportunities to support the team.

## 8. Mid-Sprint Check-in

The Scrum Master provides an asynchronous update to the Product Owner on Wednesday or Thursday through Microsoft Teams.

The update must address:

1. Which features are currently merged?
2. Which tickets are currently in QA?
3. Are there any client blockers requiring Product Owner clarification?

Team members are responsible for keeping their tasks and progress up to date so that accurate information can be communicated.

## 9. Sprint Review & Demo

The team presents its work to the Product Owner on Friday.
The review focuses on:

- Whether the application is deployed and functional.
- Whether agreed acceptance criteria have been completed.
- How the team adapted to challenges and blockers.
- Demonstration of completed functionality.
- Product Owner feedback.

The Product Owner accepts completed stories or rejects and reassigns incomplete stories to the backlog.
Relevant feedback and decisions must be documented.

## 10. Sprint Retrospective

The retrospective is an internal team meeting facilitated by the Scrum Master following the Sprint Review.
The purpose is to reflect on teamwork, collaboration and project execution.
The team follows the Start / Stop / Continue framework.

| Start | Stop | Continue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What should we introduce? | What hindered our workflow? | What worked well? |

Discussion topics may include:

- Communication.
- Collaboration.
- Task distribution.
- Sprint estimations.
- Technical challenges.
- Risk management.
- Git workflow.
- Code reviews and QA.
- Individual learning.
- Support across roles.

Agreed improvement actions should be documented and followed up during the next sprint.

## 11. Project Board & Workflow

GitHub Projects is used to maintain an organised and transparent overview of the project.

| Status | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Backlog | Work awaiting prioritisation or implementation |
| In Progress | Work currently being completed |
| In Review/QA | Work awaiting review, testing or approval |
| Done | Work completed and accepted |

All team members are responsible for updating their assigned tasks.

Work items should contain relevant:

- User story or task description.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Assignee.
- Sprint allocation.
- Estimation.
- Dependencies.
- Progress updates.

Technical contributions must follow the
[Contribution Guidelines](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## 12. Professional Conduct

To support effective collaboration and fulfil the assessment criteria, the team is expected to maintain a professional and constructive working environment.

### Respect

- Respect different perspectives, roles and experience levels.
- Allow everyone to participate in relevant discussions.
- Provide constructive feedback.
- Focus criticism on the implementation or process rather than the individual.

### Transparency

- Communicate progress and challenges honestly.
- Raise blockers and risks early.
- Document significant decisions.
- Keep assigned work visible to the team.

### Accountability

- Take responsibility for assigned tasks.
- Communicate when assistance is required.
- Follow through on agreed responsibilities.
- Maintain evidence of individual contributions.
- Participate in relevant project activities.

### Collaboration

- Support other team members where appropriate.
- Share relevant knowledge and resources.
- Work collaboratively across roles.
- Approach unfamiliar technologies and challenges as learning opportunities.

## 13. Problem-Solving & Conflict Resolution

Challenges and disagreements should be addressed through constructive communication.

The proposed resolution process is:

1. Identify and clarify the issue.
2. Communicate with the relevant team members.
3. Discuss possible solutions.
4. Request Scrum Master assistance where necessary.
5. Agree on appropriate actions.
6. Escalate unresolved concerns to the Product Owner or appropriate course staff when necessary.

The focus should remain on resolving problems, maintaining collaboration and supporting project progress.

Sensitive interpersonal concerns must not be documented in the public repository.

## 14. Risk Management

Potential risks should be identified and communicated as early as possible.

Examples include:

- Unclear requirements.
- Unrealistic sprint estimations.
- Technical blockers.
- Unfamiliar technologies.
- Delayed dependencies.
- Reduced team availability.
- Communication challenges.
- Incomplete reviews or testing.
- Deployment issues.

The team should collaboratively establish mitigation actions and adjust its approach when necessary.

The Scrum Master communicates relevant project risks to the Product Owner.

## 15. Individual Contributions & Documentation

Each team member is responsible for demonstrating their participation and contribution within their assigned role.

Evidence may include:

- GitHub commits.
- Pull requests and code reviews.
- GitHub issues and project board updates.
- Research findings and personas.
- Figma designs and design iterations.
- Testing documentation and QA results.
- SEO and analytics implementation.
- Technical documentation.
- Meeting participation.
- Problem-solving and technical decisions.

AI-assisted contributions must follow the
[AI Usage and Contribution Log](AI_LOG.md).

Documentation should accurately reflect genuine contributions and completed work.

## 16. Continuous Improvement

The team is expected to adapt its working practices throughout the project.
This includes:

- Applying feedback from the Product Owner.
- Learning from technical challenges.
- Improving collaboration and communication.
- Reviewing sprint estimations.
- Addressing workflow inefficiencies.
- Applying findings from research, testing and analytics.
- Following up on retrospective improvement actions.
